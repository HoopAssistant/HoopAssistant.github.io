Service 3 · Opponent scouting · Defense

How the opponent defends, and where to attack them

Full example: Hertener Löwen. Written defensive report and defensive video.

Pick and roll defense

From the defensive report. With their centers they alternate High Flat, Show, Drop and Under in the same game depending on the player involved. With the 4 they play Flat or switch.

Off-ball screens

  • The bigs have problems in step-up situations: sometimes they step out too high and leave a clear path to the rim, other times they don't step up at all.
  • No clear rule on when to switch on screen-the-screener actions.
  • On Chicago (pin down + hand-off) they overcommit to the first screen, which creates a big advantage on the hand-off.
  • Low post: they don't face it often; when they do, they defend 1-on-1. If a non-center posts up, they can help with the 5.

Transition defense

  • Problems in transition, especially after turnovers.
  • Aggressive going for steals: this can give advantages near the rim or open threes.
  • They struggle to match up when the offense flows quickly.
  • 2-3 zone after a free throw against Rostock, only 2 possessions.

1-on-1 defense

  • #22 Jetullahi: lateral problems in 1-on-1, especially on closeouts, and as the last defender against big centers.
  • #8 Allen: problems mainly off the ball, with positioning and rotations.
  • #44 Özmeral: strong ball pressure, but struggles to contain vertical players. Attack the rim and face him up.
  • #9 Ewald: tries to steal from behind, especially in pick and roll.
  • #11 Hinckson: their best 1-on-1 defender. Not a mismatch for guards.
